# Mobility-Aware Task Offloading in Mobile Edge Computing-Empowered Space-Air-Ground Integrated Internet-of-Things Networks

**Authors**: Minghao Sun, Jinghuai Xiong, Xiaoli Chu, Zitian Zhang

**Published**: 2026-11-10 13:56:46

**PDF URL**: [https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14102v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14102v1)

## Abstract
Mobile edge computing (MEC)-empowered spaceair-ground integrated Internet-of-Things (IoT) networks (SAGIINs) provide a promising solution for computation-intensive tasks generated by mobile IoT devices (IDs). However, task offloading in SAGIINs is challenging due to time-varying IDto-unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) channels, intermittent LEO satellite service availability, and the coupling among UAV trajectory, communication, computation, and energy decisions. This paper investigates an energy-efficient task offloading and UAV trajectory planning problem in an MEC-empowered SAGIIN, where one UAV acts as both an aerial relay and an MEC server, and multiple LEO satellites provide supplementary computing resources. The UAV trajectory planning, task splitting, and computing resource allocation are jointly optimized to minimize the total system energy consumption under task completion, communication, computation, flight region, and satellite service window constraints. The resulting non-convex problem is decomposed into a UAV trajectory planning subproblem and a task splitting and computing resource allocation subproblem. To solve the former, a retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)-assisted large language model (LLM)-enhanced deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithm, termed RLD, is proposed. The latter is shown to be convex and can be solved by using CVXPY for a given UAV trajectory. A weighted retrieval score is further designed to balance ID mobility-pattern similarity and historical UAV trajectory energy performance. Simulation results show that RLD completes all computational tasks in previously unseen test scenarios while consuming less total energy than the benchmark algorithms.

wide-area coverage, supplementary computing resources, and
resilient backhaul support when terrestrial or aerial resources
are overloaded, unavailable, or insufficient.

unstable training in large and constrained action spaces. To
overcome these limitations, large language models (LLMs)
can be used as an auxiliary source of prior knowledge for
DRL-based optimization. LLMs have demonstrated strong
capabilities in semantic understanding, reasoning, and human-
like language interaction [11]. This capability is particularly

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as
follows:
•We develop a mobility-aware MEC-empowered SAGIIN
model consisting of multiple mobile IDs, one UA V ,
and multiple LEO satellites with different service pe-
riods. Based on this model, we formulate an energy-
efficient task offloading and UA V trajectory planning
problem that minimizes the total communication, com-
puting, and UA V propulsion energy consumption sub-
ject to task-completion constraints, UA V and satellite
computing-capacity constraints, ID-to-UA V and UA V-to-
satellite transmission-capacity constraints, UA V flight-

the UA V trajectory, task splitting ratios between the UA V
and LEO satellites, and computing resources allocated to

optimization problem challenging to solve directly. To
address this, the original problem is decomposed into

DDPG for UA V trajectory planning. To enhance energy
efficiency, we design an energy-aware weighting factor

similarity between the current ID mobility pattern and
historical patterns, as well as the energy consumption
associated with the corresponding historical UA V trajec-
tories.

pletes all computational tasks and reduces the total system
energy consumption compared with benchmark algo-
rithms across all considered testing scenarios, demonstrat-
ing its effectiveness and adaptability in dynamic SAGIIN
environments. Moreover, the weighting factor analysis
confirms the effectiveness of jointly considering mobility-
pattern similarity and historical energy performance dur-
ing case retrieval in the RAG-LLM module.

II. RELATEDWORK
Existing studies have investigated task offloading and re-
source allocation in satellite edge computing from different
perspectives, including model-driven optimization methods
[13]–[17], game-theoretic approaches [18], [19], and heuristic
algorithms [20], [21]. These conventional methods usually rely
on accurate mathematical structures, fixed network assump-
tions, or repeated problem decomposition. For example, [13]
provided an effective model-driven solution for multi-UA V-
enabled SAGIIN by jointly optimizing mobile user-UA V as-
sociation, UA V trajectory, task offloading, transmit power, and
computing frequency. However, this method may suffer from
high computational complexity. In addition, [17] analyzed
an IoT-supportable satellite edge computing architecture and
investigated network slice scheduling, where latency, compu-
tational power, and transmission power were jointly optimized
to meet different IoT service requirements. However, only
a limited set of decision variables was considered, which
restricted its ability to model the complex coupling between
communication and computation in practical SAGIINs.
Heuristic algorithms and decomposition-based approaches
have also been investigated to reduce computational complex-
ity. In [20], QoS-aware multi-hop task offloading in satellite-
terrestrial edge networks was studied, and a hybrid algo-
rithm combining genetic search and the Lagrangian multiplier
method was proposed to minimize the total task latency
while satisfying QoS constraints. The authors of [21] stud-
ied LEO-satellite-assisted UA V data collection, where UA Vs
collected data from remote IDs and transmitted it through
LEO satellite relays. They jointly optimized UA V trajectory
and data transmission to reduce UA V energy consumption
using Dantzig-Wolfe decomposition, column generation, and
heuristic algorithms. Although these studies provide useful
model-driven and heuristic solutions, they are mainly based
on static system models, discretized trajectory formulations, or
limited decision variables. Therefore, they cannot fully capture
the multi-slot dynamic decision-making process, time-varying
satellite service windows, UA V trajectory-dependent channels,
or coupled communication-computation-energy constraints in
practical SAGIIN scenarios [22].
Learning-based methods have also been introduced to im-
prove the adaptability of task offloading and resource alloca-
tion in dynamic MEC systems. Authors in [23] proposed a
DRL-based method for LEO-MEC-assisted energy-harvesting
UA V systems to jointly optimize satellite selection, trans-
mission power, and partial task offloading, where an action
shaping mechanism was used to handle the mixed discrete-
continuous decision space. In [24], the authors developed a

IEEE SUBMITTED MANUSCRIPT 3
multi-heterogeneous-agent DRL framework with cross-layer
state prediction and reward redistribution to improve con-
gestion control under dynamic SAGIIN link conditions and
heterogeneous QoS requirements. In [6], the authors investi-
gated task partitioning and resource allocation, where a deep
recurrent Q-learning network was adopted to capture temporal
features and combined with federated learning to improve
decision-making efficiency. In [7], the authors proposed a
model-based two-tier RL algorithm to jointly optimize task
offloading and resource allocation in satellite-assisted IoT net-
works. In [8], the authors decomposed the long-term stochastic
computation offloading problem into deterministic per-slot
subproblems and combined DRL with traditional optimization
algorithms. In [9], the authors studied task offloading service
migration in satellite edge computing and developed a DRL-
based method to optimize offloading path selection under
dynamic service demands and constrained bandwidth, energy,
and storage resources. However, these DRL methods may
suffer from poor initial performance, inefficient exploration,
slow convergence, and unstable training when facing large,
constrained, and highly coupled action spaces.
Recently, LLMs have shown strong potential in semantic
understanding, reasoning, and decision assistance, which pro-
vides a new opportunity for enhancing DRL-based network
optimization [25]. Existing works have shown that LLMs can
reduce the sample complexity of RL in robotic manipulation
[26], guide exploration through linguistic priors and inductive
biases [27], generate meaningful goals for exploration [28],
and enhance decision-making in recommender systems, code
optimization, and information extraction tasks [12], [29], [30].
In wireless communication networks, LLMs have also been
used to analyze user objectives and constraints, select suit-
able DRL experts, assist RL-based network deployment, and
improve RL training through curriculum generation and fine-
tuning strategies [31]–[33].
Overall, most existing studies focus on either satellite-
assisted or UA V-assisted MEC systems. Although SAGIINs
have been investigated in recent works, the joint modeling of
UA V trajectory control, satellite service windows, parallel task
splitting, and communication-computation-energy coupling is
still limited. More importantly, the integrated mobility of IDs,
UA Vs, and LEO satellites has not been sufficiently addressed.
This limitation becomes more critical in emerging scenarios
such as the low-altitude economy, where the movement of
ground devices, aerial platforms, and satellites can jointly
reshape the communication links, computing opportunities,
and service availability.

V. ALGORITHMDESIGN
In this section, we present the proposed RLD algorithm
for solving the formulated EE-TOTD problem. The original
problem is decomposed into two subproblems. The UA V
trajectory planning subproblem is solved by using an RAG-
assisted LLM-DDPG method, while the task splitting and
computing resource allocation subproblem is solved using
CVXPY . Based on the solutions to the two subproblems,
an alternating optimization algorithm is devised to solve the
formulated EE-TOTD problem.

The RLD algorithm comprises three interconnected mod-
ules: a case-based RAG-LLM module, a DDPG learning
module, and a CVXPY-based resource optimization module.

VII. CONCLUSIONS
In this paper, we investigated energy-efficient task offload-
ing and UA V trajectory planning in an MEC-empowered
SAGIIN with mobile IDs and time-varying LEO satellite
service windows. We formulated a joint optimization problem
for UA V trajectory planning, task splitting, and computing
resource allocation, and decomposed it into two subproblems.
A RLD algorithm was proposed for UA V trajectory planning,
while the task splitting and computing resource allocation
subproblem was reformulated as a convex problem and solved
using CVXPY . An energy-aware weighted retrieval mechanism
was designed to balance mobility-pattern similarity and his-
torical energy performance, providing informative guidance
for DDPG exploration. Simulation results demonstrated that
RLD achieves lower energy consumption and maintains task
feasibility across different mobility scenarios and ID densities.
It also provides substantially lower online decision latency
than conventional search-based algorithms. The weight-factor
analysis further validates the effectiveness of the proposed re-
trieval mechanism, highlighting the potential of RAG-assisted
DRL for energy-efficient task offloading in dynamic SAGIINs.
